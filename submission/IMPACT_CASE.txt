TARGET MILESTONE
Help a patient organization reach an expert-reviewed feasibility decision about
adapting an existing natural-history framework or using relevant research data.
This is one milestone on the route to treatment research. An editable brief is
an intermediate artifact; faster writing alone does not prove a faster study,
trial, treatment or cure.

BASELINE PATH
The organization searches publications and program pages, identifies relevant
resources, separates documented use from gaps, checks access requirements,
locates appropriate public collaborator routes, and drafts a sourced inquiry.
Researchers must still check applicability, consent/access, implementation,
measurement properties and the proposed cohort. Those reviews can dominate the
time to the actual feasibility milestone.

PROPOSED PATH
RareBridge organizes that same source set into explained routes and use-specific
checks. Sources remain inspectable; duplicate identifiers remain one paper.
Unanswered checks become concrete questions with collaborator roles. A reviewed
brief can make the first expert conversation more focused and reduce repeated
discovery or requests for missing information. This is the proposed mechanism of
benefit, to be measured rather than assumed.

SMALL PILOT: OBSERVABLE INTERMEDIATE OUTCOME
Task: prepare a first collaboration brief for a SYNGAP1 organization considering
the published STARR/ProMMiS framework for a natural-history study.
Use the same frozen source set and identical fictional local-cohort assumptions
for both methods. Label the assumptions; do not invent real participants.
Completion requires all of these:
  - Correct target, goal, resource and scoped proposed use.
  - Accurate supported/unknown checks, including access and local fit.
  - Source URLs and no duplicated-paper evidence inflation.
  - At least two concrete unanswered questions and responsible roles.
  - A sourced public collaborator route and a reviewable draft message.
  - No claim of approved reuse, established SCN2A fit or treatment benefit.

Manual method: source pack/browser plus a blank text editor, no RareBridge.
App method: the integrated app using the same pack, including its visible gaps.
Start timing when the participant receives the task. Stop when the brief passes
the completion checklist. Record active work and elapsed wall time separately.
Both methods include source inspection and corrections. Record failures/omissions
rather than silently excluding a slow or incomplete run.
Prefer two participants with reversed order to reduce practice effects. If time
allows only one participant, report a one-person pilot and the order limitation.
Do not compare an expert's app use with a beginner's manual task as if controlled.
Review briefs without knowing the method when practical. No patient data needed.

THE 10X AMBITION: EXPLICIT ASSUMPTIONS
The challenge asks for acceleration of a meaningful treatment-path milestone.
Our ambition is a shorter route to a feasibility decision through reuse and
better prepared collaboration. Use estimates only as scenarios, not findings.

Let D = manual discovery/preparation time, R = partner response and expert-review
time, F = follow-up/corrections time. Baseline milestone time is D + R + F.
Let D_app, R_app and F_app denote the same components with RareBridge.
Potential milestone acceleration = (D + R + F) / (D_app + R_app + F_app).
Fill every component from observation or a named stakeholder estimate. If only
D was measured, the milestone ratio remains unknown. If R is unchanged and
dominates, a large preparation gain may produce only a small overall gain.
Reusing an established framework could avoid some new design work, but estimate
that only with investigator input; the hackathon does not validate it.

If discovery/preparation includes follow-up and partner/expert review remains r,
10x requires (D_manual + r) / (D_app + r) >= 10, or
D_manual >= 10 * D_app + 9 * r. Purely illustrative, not observed: D_app=1 abstract
unit and r=1 unit require D_manual>=19 units. These are hypothetical units, not
claimed hours or actual impact evidence.

DIFFERENTIATION BOUNDARY
Disease graphs, resource directories, partner networks and outcome-measure
adaptation already exist. RareBridge's contribution is their integration into
an inspectable proposed-use workflow: explain a candidate, assess recorded
support, expose source dependencies, identify gaps, and prepare a sourced next
action. The STXBP1/SYNGAP1 published link validates the workflow; it is not our
discovery. Candidate suggestions and new extraction remain qualified/pending.
